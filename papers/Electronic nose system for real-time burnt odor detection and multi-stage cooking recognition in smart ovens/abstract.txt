Burnt odors, volatile organic compounds (VOCs) released by overcooked food, serve as early indicators of kitchen fire hazards. However, existing gas sensing technologies lack the capability to detect burnt odors across diverse cooking scenarios. We present an electronic nose (E-nose) system integrated with a household oven for multi-stage cooking recognition. The E-nose comprises four metal-oxide semiconductor (MOS) gas sensors coupled with a water-cooling gas-path structure designed to condition the humidity and oil contamination of oven gas. Four representative recipes covering common food categories were selected to evaluate system performance. The proposed system continuously monitors the entire roasting process, which is further defined into four stages: raw, tender, crispy, and burnt. By employing a window-based feature extraction strategy combined with the Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) algorithm, the system achieved approximately 90% classification accuracy in both single-device and cross-device offline evaluations. Furthermore, the trained model was deployed on an embedded board within a new E-nose device for real-time inference across 20 intensive cooking trials, yielding an average accuracy of 91.3% and demonstrating initial stability of the system. Additionally, unseen-recipe validation complemented by trend analysis on six additional recipes provided preliminary evidence of the system’s generalizability to novel ingredients. This work offers a promising technology for applying electronic nose in smart kitchen safety monitoring and intelligent home appliances.
