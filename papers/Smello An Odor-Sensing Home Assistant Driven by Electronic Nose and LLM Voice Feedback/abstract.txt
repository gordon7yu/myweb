Machine olfaction, despite its significance as a sensing modality, remains largely underexplored in the scope of human–machine interaction. Recent advances in electronic noses (e-noses) have enabled smart systems to detect and classify odors, yet the interaction ends at the sensing side, leaving users without meaningful, actionable, or affectively aligned feedback. This limitation is particularly salient in domestic environments, where olfactory cues span a wide spectrum from hazardous to comforting. A central challenge therefore emerges: how might raw odor signals be translated into situationally relevant and emotionally attuned feedback? To address this challenge, we introduce Smello, an large language models -driven voice assistant designed for closed-loop odor-sensing feedback. Smello combines a compact four-sensor e-nose with a five-layer reasoning architecture to support real-time odor recognition and the generation of contextualized verbal responses. Users can interact with the system through real odor samples and open-ended dialogue. To examine Smello’s odor-to-feedback translation capability and to gain insights into how users interact with an odor-aware assistant, we selected four representative household odors that cover different pleasantness and risk levels and conducted a user study with 32 participants. Smello recognized odors and consistently produced clear, contextually appropriate feedback. The study further revealed diverse user preferences regarding information granularity and emotional support, as well as scenario-dependent mechanisms influencing trust formation and motivation to act.
